08 — Shapes: decision diamond, start/end pill, circle

The three approved outlines (components.md § Shapes), each in two or three options, in Architect's look on the dotted canvas. Lines are computed, not drawn by hand: they leave from a port and stop at the outline's edge, exactly as routeLinks will. Section 3's circle links are straight, centre to centre. Toggle dark and light at the right.

1 — Decision diamond, in a small flow

One way in at the top, a "yes" and a "no" way out. The same flow three times; the lone shape on the right of each stage shows what a long title does to it.

A — True diamond · title inside, short titles

Suits a one- or two-word question ("Signed in?", "Paid plan?") and the classic flowchart reading: the shape says fork before you read it.
  • Risk: only the middle half of the box holds text. A 41-character title needs a 240 × 240 diamond (right) against a 280 × 122 card, so neighbours are pushed apart and there is no room for body text or the kind tab.
  • The number tab sits on the upper-left edge; the kind is a small word inside, since the top point belongs to the incoming line.

B — Fork card · card body, diamond badge, ports on the sides

Suits real sentences: the agent's usual titles and a line of context, in the card everyone already knows, so wrapping, tabs, size tiers and the footer all keep working.
  • Risk: the signal is smaller: a 30px badge in the kind tab's slot plus two port dots, not a silhouette. The entry line meets the badge's top point.
  • A long title just wraps and the card grows taller (right); width stays on its tier.

C — Hexagon · points left and right, flat top and bottom

Suits a title of up to about 50 characters: the text area is 70% of the width, so the same footprint as a small card holds two lines. Exits leave the two points; the entry meets the flat top.
  • Risk: in classic flowcharts a hexagon means "preparation", not a decision, so first-time readers may not see a fork. It adds a fourth outline the spec has not approved.
Recommendation: A if the agent is told to keep fork titles to about 16 characters; otherwise B. C only if the diamond's shape cost worries you more than its meaning.

2 — Start and end pills

A flow that begins and ends in a pill. Pills have two ports, the rounded ends (left and right here; top and bottom in a vertical flow), and the arrow stops at the end of the curve.

A — Plain capsule · icon and title, nothing on the border

Suits terminals that are only a signpost: "Start", "Video plays", "Done". Smallest footprint, and it cannot be mistaken for a card.
  • Risk: no number and no kind tab, so it cannot be cited ("item 3"), noted, or get a proposal tab on its border. A long title wraps into a taller stadium (below) with the text area shrinking at both ends.

B — Capsule with a kind tab · number and START / END tab, like cards

Suits every pill that is a real node of the board: it has a number for references, a tab for the kind, and room for a note pin, so proposals and notes work the same as on cards.
  • Risk: 12px taller once the tabs straddle the border, and a START tab on a 190px pill is a lot of label for one word.
Recommendation: B, because notes, proposals and "item 3" references hang off the tabs; use A only for pills that are drawn but not addressable.

3 — Circles in a radial view

A centre circle with the things around it. Each circle is sized to its text (the ruler at the top left is measured live), and every link is a straight line from centre to centre that stops at the rim, or at the box edge of a card.

A — All circles · mind map, short labels

Suits noun labels of one to three words ("Brand image" and where it is used). Shown: centre with number and kind tab; Social posts selected (1.5px, its own colour); Email signature with a note pin; Partner co-marketing templates at the largest size.
  • Risk: the diameter follows the text, so area grows about with the square of the length: 8 characters is 84px, 30 characters 140px. The ring must widen to keep gaps, and there is no room for body text; detail would have to open on select.
  • Tabs sit on the rim: number at upper left, kind tab on top (only when the circle is wide enough), pin at upper right.

B — Centre circle, card children · for children that carry text

Suits a radial view whose satellites carry a sentence or bullets, so the people reading can see the detail without opening anything. The line still runs centre to centre; it stops at the circle's rim on one end and the card's box edge on the other.
  • Risk: two outlines in one view, and at 7 or more cards the box-edge clipping leaves uneven line lengths. Cards do not shrink to the ring, so the ring is larger (about 860 × 420 here).
Recommendation: A for pure labels (the brand-image example); B when children have body text. Make it the agent's choice, like the layout mode.
